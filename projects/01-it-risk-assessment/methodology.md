# Assessment Methodology

## 1. Purpose

Our purpose is to provide a repeatable and transparent method for assessing technology and cybersecurity risks in the simulated case study. This document is educational only and does not constitute an audit opinion, certification, or legal/compliance advice for anyone but the students.

## 2. Risk statement format

Use: **Because of [condition], [threat/event] could occur, leading to [business impact].**

A useful risk statement describes a business consequence, not only a technical weakness.

## 3. Scoring model

Score likelihood and impact from 1 to 5.

### Likelihood

| Score | Label | General interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rare | Exceptional circumstances |
| 2 | Unlikely | Not expected, but possible |
| 3 | Possible | Could occur under plausible conditions |
| 4 | Likely | Expected to occur in many circumstances |
| 5 | Almost Certain | Expected frequently or imminently |

### Impact

| Score | Label | General interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Insignificant | Minimal disruption or loss |
| 2 | Minor | Limited impact, readily recoverable |
| 3 | Moderate | Material but manageable disruption or loss |
| 4 | Major | Significant business, customer, legal or operational impact |
| 5 | Severe | Critical disruption, extensive exposure or major business harm |

**Inherent risk score = Likelihood × Impact**

| Score | Rating |
|---|---|
| 1-4 | Low |
| 5-9 | Moderate |
| 10-15 | High |
| 16-25 | Critical |

These thresholds are illustrative. A real organization should approve its risk matrix and impact criteria.

## 4. Inherent and residual risk

- **Inherent risk:** exposure before considering the effect of controls.
- **Residual risk:** exposure remaining after relevant controls and treatment actions are considered.

Residual ratings in this case study are planning estimates only. They must be reassessed after controls are implemented and validated; they are not calculated automatically from the inherent score.

## 5. Control assessment

Assess both:

- **Design effectiveness:** whether the control, if implemented as designed, could address the stated risk.
- **Operating effectiveness:** whether reliable evidence shows that the control operated as intended over the relevant period.

A policy document alone does not prove operating effectiveness.

Illustrative statuses:
- **Effective:** sufficient design and operating evidence supports the control.
- **Partially Effective:** a control exists but coverage, consistency or execution is incomplete.
- **Needs Improvement:** the control is absent, inadequately designed, immature or unsupported by sufficient evidence.
- **Not Assessed:** evidence or scope is insufficient to reach a conclusion.

For this simulated project, status labels are hypotheses for demonstrating workpaper design. No real control effectiveness has been verified.

## 6. Evidence principles

For each control, record:
1. evidence requested,
2. evidence owner,
3. period covered,
4. completeness and reliability,
5. test performed,
6. result and exceptions,
7. reviewer and date.

Do not mark a control effective merely because evidence was requested or a policy exists.

## 7. Risk treatment

Treatment options include:
- **Mitigate:** reduce likelihood and/or impact.
- **Avoid:** stop the activity creating the exposure.
- **Transfer/share:** allocate part of the exposure contractually or through insurance; this does not eliminate accountability.
- **Accept:** obtain documented approval from an authorized risk owner within risk appetite.

## 8. Remediation closure

A remediation item should be closed only when:
- the action is implemented,
- supporting evidence is attached or referenced,
- validation/testing is completed,
- exceptions are documented,
- residual risk is reassessed,
- the designated owner approves closure.

## 9. Framework mapping

NIST CSF 2.0 and ISO/IEC 27001:2022 references are used to organize control discussion. Mapping does not establish compliance by itself. Applicability and exact control interpretation must be confirmed against the authoritative framework text and the organization's scope.
